# Research brief

**Setting.** Work teams in a large multi-site manufacturing and services firm.

**Research question.** What determines team performance in this firm, and in particular how does team turnover affect team performance, through which mechanisms, and under what conditions?

**Data.** `data.csv` with N = 1200 observations at the team-year level. See the codebook for variables.

**Prior literature available to the team** (summaries of earlier studies in comparable settings; cite them by ID):

- **[L1]** Panel of 212 manufacturing teams over four years. Higher share of team members who left and were replaced during the year was associated with lower supervisor-rated team performance against annual targets (standardized b = -0.15, p < .01). The authors attribute the pattern to the loss of tacit, team-specific knowledge.
- **[L2]** Field study of 96 software teams with quarterly skills audits. Higher share of team members who left and were replaced during the year was associated with higher loss of team-specific procedural knowledge, from quarterly skills audits (standardized b = +0.24, p < .01).
- **[L3]** Survey of 340 service teams in two retail chains. The association between share of team members who left and were replaced during the year and supervisor-rated team performance against annual targets was more positive when degree to which the team's work procedures are documented in written routines was high (interaction b = +0.34, p < .05). Documented routines are argued to buffer teams against the disruption of member exit.
- **[L4]** Cross-sectional study of 1,105 teams in a logistics firm. Higher hours of formal training purchased for the team was associated with higher supervisor-rated team performance against annual targets (standardized b = +0.21, p < .01). The authors recommend expanding formal training budgets.
- **[L5]** Two-wave survey of 180 hospital units. Higher average weekly workload per member relative to the firm norm was associated with lower supervisor-rated team performance against annual targets (standardized b = -0.24, p < .01).
- **[L6]** Meta-analytic summary of 41 team studies. Units with higher number of team members showed no significant difference in supervisor-rated team performance against annual targets (standardized b = +0.03, n.s.).
- **[L7]** Archival study of 2,300 hires in a professional services firm. Higher quality of replacement hires, from structured assessment scores at entry was associated with higher supervisor-rated team performance against annual targets (standardized b = +0.17, p < .01).

**Deliverable.** A set of causal claims about what determines the focal outcome, each stated with the exact variable names in the codebook, a calibrated confidence in [0, 1], and its evidential basis (data analysis, literature IDs, or both). Claims may assert positive, negative or no effects, moderation (whether the effect of a cause on an outcome becomes more positive or more negative as a moderator increases), or mediation (a cause acting on an outcome through a mediator).
