# Research brief

**Setting.** Operating units of a large knowledge-intensive services firm.

**Research question.** What determines creative output in these units, and in particular how does the intensity of electronic performance monitoring affect creative output, through which mechanisms, and under what conditions?

**Data.** `data.csv` with N = 1200 observations at the unit-year level. See the codebook for variables.

**Prior literature available to the team** (summaries of earlier studies in comparable settings; cite them by ID):

- **[L1]** Multi-level study of 310 units in a financial services firm. Higher intensity of electronic performance monitoring of employees in the unit was associated with lower number and quality of novel solutions and process improvements generated by the unit, judged by an expert panel (standardized b = -0.22, p < .01). Monitoring is argued to crowd out intrinsic motivation and exploratory behavior.
- **[L2]** Three-wave survey of 1,480 employees in 120 units. Higher intensity of electronic performance monitoring of employees in the unit was associated with lower employees' reported discretion over how they do their work (standardized b = -0.31, p < .01).
- **[L3]** Survey of 204 R&D units with structural equation modeling. The effect of intensity of electronic performance monitoring of employees in the unit on number and quality of novel solutions and process improvements generated by the unit, judged by an expert panel was statistically mediated by employees' reported discretion over how they do their work (indirect effect significant at p < .05).
- **[L4]** Cross-sectional study of 890 units across 14 firms. Higher scope of employee wellness programs offered by the unit was associated with higher number and quality of novel solutions and process improvements generated by the unit, judged by an expert panel (standardized b = +0.17, p < .01). The authors argue that well-being frees cognitive resources for creative work.
- **[L5]** Natural experiment around the staggered rollout of a monitoring system in 62 call centers. Higher intensity of electronic performance monitoring of employees in the unit was associated with higher volume of standard tasks completed per employee (standardized b = +0.30, p < .01).
- **[L6]** Archival study of 2,100 supervisor-unit observations. Units with higher number of direct reports per supervisor showed no significant difference in number and quality of novel solutions and process improvements generated by the unit, judged by an expert panel (standardized b = +0.01, n.s.).
- **[L7]** Panel of 150 units over six years. Higher average employee tenure in the unit was associated with higher number and quality of novel solutions and process improvements generated by the unit, judged by an expert panel (standardized b = +0.25, p < .01).

**Deliverable.** A set of causal claims about what determines the focal outcome, each stated with the exact variable names in the codebook, a calibrated confidence in [0, 1], and its evidential basis (data analysis, literature IDs, or both). Claims may assert positive, negative or no effects, moderation (whether the effect of a cause on an outcome becomes more positive or more negative as a moderator increases), or mediation (a cause acting on an outcome through a mediator).
